Part One — Product

0Why This Document Exists

RAWINDIA has been built one feature at a time — a spin decoder here, a sparring partner there, a chatbot bolted onto the archive — and it shows. The site currently reads as a pile of demos, not a product with a point of view.

Three specific complaints drove this redesign, and they're worth stating plainly rather than smoothing over:

  • It's jittery. Features were added independently, each with its own modal, its own visual language, its own reason for existing. Nothing ties them together.
  • Some of it is gimmick. Argument Sparring Partner and Chat With The Archive are both chat interfaces bolted onto a news site. Anyone who genuinely wants to interrogate an argument or ask an open-ended question already has ChatGPT or Claude open in another tab — full-strength, no local-model quality ceiling, no waiting on CPU inference. A worse copy of that experience, running on a news site, gets used once out of curiosity and never again. That's the definition of a gimmick.
  • Some of it is generic. A live wire, a search bar, a taxonomy explorer — competent, but indistinguishable from any other India news aggregator. Nothing here is a reason to come back.

The fix isn't a seventh feature bolted onto the pile. It's picking one idea sharp enough to be worth building a whole product around, and cutting everything that doesn't serve it.

The one idea RAWINDIA is not a newsroom. It's a ledger — a running, public, receipts-first record of what India's institutions and politicians said they'd do, and whether they actually did it. Every other feature either feeds that ledger or gets cut.

1Product Vision & Positioning

Positioning statement: RAWINDIA is the place that keeps score — a public W/L record for every promise, deadline, and spin attempt an Indian politician or institution puts on the record, built to be screenshotted and forwarded, not read end-to-end.

This is a real, validated genre, not an invented one. India already has ManifestoWatch, Sarkari Vaade and Jumlameter tracking manifesto promises against gazette/budget records, and PolitiFact's Truth-O-Meter has run the same playbook on U.S. presidents for over a decade with a five-way verdict scale (Kept / Broken / Compromise / Stalled / In the Works) built entirely on verifiable outcomes, never on stated intent. Separately, Gen Z's actual political information diet is already meme-native — researchers tracking 2026's youth protest movements describe memes as a primary vehicle for political criticism and accountability, not a joke layered on top of it.

RAWINDIA's opening isn't inventing the promise-tracker — it's translating one into the visual and sharing language a scroll-first, WhatsApp-forwarding, Instagram-story audience already speaks: a stamped verdict, a running clock, a report card — not a table in a PDF.

What RAWINDIA explicitly is not

  • Not a chatbot. No feature should require a reader to type an open-ended question and wait for a generated answer. If the answer needs to come from typing a question, the feature has already lost to ChatGPT.
  • Not neutral wire-service voice. The ledger has a stance: results over intentions, receipts over rhetoric.
  • Not a legal fact-checker. RAWINDIA doesn't claim adjudicating authority — it claims to link its sources and let the reader judge. See §5.

2The Ledger Reader

Primary persona 19–29, urban or semi-urban India. Politically aware, but exhausted by both mainstream-media spin and preachy fact-check essays. Consumes news in a scroll, not a sit-down read. Trusts a sharp, sourced, peer-shared take over a 1,200-word explainer. Shares things via WhatsApp status and Instagram stories, not by emailing a link. Wants the receipts, not the op-ed.

This reader doesn't need RAWINDIA to explain why a promise was broken in six paragraphs. They need to see the stamp, the source, and the clock — and a card worth forwarding to the group chat.

4.1The L/W Ledger

A running feed where every major policy move, court verdict, or political event gets exactly one verdict: a W for whoever it favored, an L for whoever it cost. Blunt on purpose — the ledger doesn't have room for "it's complicated."

L — Fiscal Hawks
Union Budget hikes subsidy outlay 18%, deficit target slips to 5.1%
Source: PIB Budget Statement, 1 Feb 2026
W — Commuters
Delhi–Meerut RRTS full corridor opens, cuts travel time to 55 min
Source: NCRTC press release, 12 Aug 2026

Editorial rule: a verdict is assigned to the outcome, never the person — "L for commuters" is fair game, "L for [name], who is clearly incompetent" is not. See §5.

Why this beats a chatbot: zero typing, zero waiting on a model. A reader gets the take in the time it takes to glance at a card — and every card is built to be forwarded as-is.

4.2The Vaada Clock

This is the literal feature that was asked for: every tracked promise gets a running clock, and every deadline that gets pushed stays on the record instead of quietly disappearing.

"Every household in the state will have a piped water connection by March 2023."
Mar 2023→Dec 2024→Jun 2027 Extended ×2
1,247days overdue on the original deadline

The status taxonomy is deliberately not invented from scratch — it adapts the five-way scale PolitiFact has run for over a decade, because "requires a verifiable outcome, not stated intent" is the one rule every real promise-tracker in the research converged on independently:

StatusMeaning
KeptVerifiable outcome delivered, matching the original commitment.
BrokenAbandoned, contradicted, or missed with no path forward.
ExtendedDeadline pushed at least once. Full push history stays visible — this is the clock.
StalledNo measurable movement; no new deadline set either.
In ProgressActive, on-schedule movement toward the original or current deadline.

Non-negotiable rule, carried over from every precedent studied (ManifestoWatch, Sarkari Vaade, Jumlameter, PolitiFact): no card renders without at least one linked source per status change. An unsourced verdict is worse than no verdict — see §5 and §10.

4.3Netaji Report Card v2

One page per tracked politician or institution, replacing the current split between the Spin Leaderboard and the Institutional Report Card with a single scorecard:

  • W/L tally — rolled up from every L/W Ledger entry naming them.
  • Vaada record — kept / broken / extended / stalled counts, pulled straight from §4.2's data.
  • Spin frequency — reused as-is from the existing Spin Decoder ledger (no rework needed, it already logs exactly this).
  • Hard facts (Phase 2, not promised for launch) — assets and pending criminal cases, sourced from ADR's public candidate-affidavit data (myneta.info) if a genuinely free access path is confirmed. Flagged explicitly as unconfirmed rather than assumed — see §15.

Exportable as a single shareable "report card" image via the same engine described next.

4.4Verdict Card Engine

Infrastructure, not a reader-facing feature by itself — but the direct fix for the "jittery architecture" complaint. Today, Quote Cards are a single-purpose canvas generator wired to one modal. It becomes one shared engine, parameterized by verdict type, used everywhere a screenshot-worthy card is needed:

renderVerdictCard({
  type: 'quote' | 'lw-verdict' | 'promise-status' | 'report-card',
  // shared brand chrome (masthead, colors, watermark) — always applied
  ...typeSpecificFields
})

One component, one visual identity, reused four ways — instead of four modals each reinventing card layout, brand colors, and export logic independently.

5Editorial Guardrails

Naming real, named, living politicians in a "broken promise" ledger is the single highest-risk surface in this entire redesign — get it wrong and the failure mode isn't a bad UX review, it's a defamation claim. Three rules are non-negotiable, not aspirational:

  1. No verdict without a source link. Every status, every W/L, every "extended" — one click away from the actual evidence. This isn't a nice-to-have; it's what separates a ledger from a rumor mill, and it's the one rule every real-world precedent (§1) shares without exception.
  2. Two-tier trust badge, always visible. Anything an LLM proposed and a human hasn't reviewed yet is marked AI-Flagged · Unverified. Nothing upgrades to RAWINDIA Verified without passing through the review queue in §10. These badges render on the card itself, not just in an admin view — readers should never be able to mistake one for the other.
  3. Mock the action, never the person. "L for the water ministry" is a fair, sourced take on an outcome. Commentary on a politician's appearance, family, or character has no place on the ledger — it's not the point, and it's the fastest way to turn a credible accountability project into a target.

6Success Metrics & Non-Goals

No analytics budget exists, so metrics have to be self-tracked in-app rather than pulled from a paid dashboard:

  • Verdict cards exported/shared (a simple counter on the export action)
  • Report Card page visits vs. general article visits (relative, not absolute — signal of whether the ledger is the draw)
  • Returning-visitor rate via a lightweight localStorage-based marker

Non-goals

  • Not attempting newsroom-scale, legally-adjudicated fact-checking.
  • Not covering every sitting politician on day one — launch with a focused, named top-N list (ministers/parties actually appearing in the existing ingested news pool) and grow it deliberately.
  • Not building real user authentication for the review queue — see §10's explicit scope limit.
Part Two — Technical

7Constraints, Restated Plainly

Everything below is designed against exactly what's actually available — nothing assumes a budget that doesn't exist:

  • Groq API — existing free-tier key(s), hard-capped at ~8,000 tokens per request. Already fully committed to the must-run article-rewrite pipeline; the new promise-extraction workload must not compete with it for that budget.
  • Local Ollama — qwen2.5:7b-instruct (chat) + nomic-embed-text (embeddings), CPU inference, GPU-offload already enabled. Free, but slow and with a real quality ceiling on structured extraction — this is exactly why §10's human review queue is mandatory, not optional.
  • No dedicated backend. Persistence stays in browser-local IndexedDB; server logic stays in stateless Vercel serverless functions with in-memory (cold-start-reset) rate limiting, matching every existing proxy in api/.
  • Single operator. There is no editorial desk. Any human-in-the-loop step has to be a two-minute approve/edit/reject action, not a workflow.

9Extraction Pipeline

A new background service, promiseExtractionService.ts, runs entirely on local Ollama — the Groq token budget stays reserved for the existing rewrite pipeline. It runs after an article has already passed quality-filtering, deduplication, and Groq rewrite (i.e. only on the same small, already-vetted stream everything else in the app already processes — not the raw multi-source firehose).

For each article, one structured-JSON Ollama pass asks two questions: does this contain a new dated commitment from a named politician or institution, and does it reference or update an existing tracked promise (matched via the reused embedding-similarity primitives from the former archive-chat infrastructure — see §13)? Either answer produces a candidate record — never a published one.

Why nothing here auto-publishes: a 7B local model making a structured factual claim about a real, named politician — a date, a deadline, a status — is exactly the kind of output that looks confident and is sometimes wrong. With no fact-checking budget to catch that after the fact, the only responsible design is: every extraction lands in the review queue, and nothing reaches a reader until a human has looked at the actual source excerpt next to the claim.

10Review Queue

A new, unlinked route — /ops/review — gated by a passphrase compared client-side against a Vite env var. This is explicitly not real authentication; it's a deterrent appropriate to a zero-budget, single-operator tool, and nothing more sensitive than a moderation queue should ever sit behind it.

Each queued item shows the model's proposed record side-by-side with the exact source excerpt it was drawn from, with inline-editable fields and three actions: Approve (flips trustTier to Verified and publishes), Edit & Approve, or Reject (discarded, never shown).

12Card Engine — Component Sketch

Extends the existing canvas-based quoteCardService.ts rather than replacing it — same brand chrome, same export mechanism, new type-specific layout branches:

interface VerdictCardInput {
  type: 'quote' | 'lw-verdict' | 'promise-status' | 'report-card';
  subjectName: string;
  headline: string;
  verdict?: 'W' | 'L' | Promise['status'];
  sourceUrl: string;
  trustTier: 'ai-flagged' | 'verified';
}

function renderVerdictCard(input: VerdictCardInput): HTMLCanvasElement

Same export mechanism as today (canvas → PNG blob → native share sheet / download), so nothing about the sharing UX itself needs to change — only the range of things it can render.

15Risks & Open Questions

Risk — model reliability A local 7B model will misread dates, misattribute a quote, or invent a deadline that sounds plausible. Mitigated structurally, not by hoping the model improves: nothing self-publishes (§9), every card carries its trust tier (§5), every verdict carries a source link.
Risk — defamation exposure Naming real politicians in a "broken promise" ledger is the whole point, but also the whole legal risk. Mitigated by §5's guardrails and by keeping every verdict tied to outcomes, not character judgments.
Open question — not yet confirmed Whether ADR/myneta.info exposes a genuinely free, usable data path (API or bulk download) for the Phase 4 hard-facts enrichment hasn't been verified yet. Treated as unconfirmed and out of scope for Phases 0–3 rather than assumed available.
Risk — single-operator bottleneck One person reviewing every extraction doesn't scale past a handful of items a day. By design, the queue only ever sees the same small, already-filtered article stream the rest of the app processes — not a raw firehose — to keep the review workload realistic for one operator.
